About Kondapalli Toys

Kondapalli Bommalu are handcrafted wooden toys from Kondapalli village, near Vijayawada in Andhra Pradesh. Made by hand for about 400 years, they are one of India's best-known toy-making traditions and have a Geographical Indication (GI) tag that marks them as a genuine product of Kondapalli.

A 400-Year-Old Heritage

Local tradition says the craft was brought to Kondapalli by families of artisans, the Aryakshatriyas (Nakarshalu), who settled near the Kondapalli hills and fort. Their skills have been passed from parent to child for generations, and the same families still carve the toys today. The toys show village life, gods and goddesses, animals and stories from the epics. Favourites include the Ambari elephant, the bullock cart, the Dasavataram set and the dancing doll. Many homes in South India display them in the Bommala Koluvu (doll display) during Sankranti and Dasara.

Made from Tella Poniki Chekka

Every toy at Krishna Toys is carved from Tella Poniki chekka, a soft, light wood from the trees of the Kondapalli hills. Because the wood is soft, artisans can carve fine details by hand. Because it is light, the finished toys are easy to handle and safe for children. This local wood is what gives Kondapalli toys their own look and feel.

How Each Toy Is Made

  1. Carving: Tella Poniki wood is cut and carved by hand into separate parts, such as the body, head and limbs.
  2. Joining: the parts are fixed together with makku, a traditional paste of tamarind seed powder and sawdust, which is also used to shape fine details.
  3. Smoothing: the edges are finished smooth and a base coat is applied.
  4. Painting: the toy is painted in bright colours with water colours, vegetable dyes or enamel paints, depending on the toy.
